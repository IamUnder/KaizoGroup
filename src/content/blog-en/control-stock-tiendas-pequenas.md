In a small shop, "keeping track of inventory" almost always starts with good intentions — a spreadsheet, a careful initial count — and gets abandoned within a few weeks, because nobody has time to update it by hand every time something sells. The problem isn't a lack of discipline: it's that inventory that doesn't update itself with every sale is doomed to go stale.

## First: selling has to deduct stock, no exceptions

If every POS sale or every invoice to a customer doesn't automatically deduct from the corresponding item's stock, any number you look at in inventory is, at best, a snapshot from a while back — not reality. This is by far the most important piece: without it, everything else (low-stock alerts, purchase forecasting, whatever else) is built on data that's already wrong.

## Next: know what's come in, not just what's gone out

Once sales are correctly deducting stock, the next step is logging incoming stock — what arrives with each supplier order. You don't need a complex purchasing system: it's enough for every delivery to be logged (which item, how much, when), so that available stock is the real sum of what came in minus what went out, not a figure someone updates "when they remember."

## One warehouse, or several — but the tool shouldn't force the choice

Many small shops have a single location and don't need to think about "warehouses" at all. The key is that the system doesn't complicate your life if you only have one shop (it should work with a single default warehouse, no friction), but also doesn't leave you stuck the day you open a second location or rent storage space — at that point, adding a second warehouse and transferring stock between them should be simple, not a migration.

## The till and the invoice have to speak the same stock language

If you sell both over the counter (POS, anonymous customer) and to customers you work with regularly (invoiced to their name), both need to deduct from the same stock. It's a common mistake to treat these as separate systems — the result is inventory that never balances because part of your sales never gets reflected.

## What can wait

You don't need demand forecasting or advanced turnover reports from day one. With stock updated in real time and a clear record of what's come in, you already have the foundation for better purchasing decisions — any more advanced automation only makes sense once that basic data is reliable.
