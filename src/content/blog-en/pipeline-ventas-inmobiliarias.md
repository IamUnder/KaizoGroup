In real estate, the difference between an opportunity that closes and one that falls through the cracks is almost never the client's actual interest — it's the follow-up. A conversation left sitting in an agent's WhatsApp, a contact nobody follows up with after a first viewing, a proposal sent that nobody remembers to check on two weeks later. The problem isn't sales — it's organization.

## A contact and an opportunity aren't the same thing (and mixing them up costs money)

A contact is a person: an interested buyer, an owner who wants to sell. An opportunity is a specific process with that contact — the sale of a particular property, with its own estimated value and its own stage. The same buyer can have several opportunities open at once (they're looking at three different flats), and treating them as one thing blends the information together and makes it impossible to know how much business you actually have open.

## A visual board, not a list you have to read end to end

The difference between a pipeline that works and one that doesn't usually comes down to whether it's visual. A board by stage (New → Contacted → Proposal sent → Negotiation → Won/Lost), where each opportunity is a card you drag from one column to the next, lets you see at a glance where business is getting stuck — if ten cards have piled up in "Contacted" without moving, that's a clear signal something needs a push, without having to read a full list to notice.

## Viewings are part of the history too, not a separate event

When a property viewing gets booked in one tool and the follow-up conversation lives in another, context gets lost. If the appointment is linked directly to the contact and the opportunity, any agent picking up that conversation immediately sees: when the last viewing was, what was discussed, and what stage the process is at — without depending on whoever did the viewing remembering to tell everyone else.

## How much your open business is worth, without exporting anything to a spreadsheet

A question every agency asks constantly is "how's the month going?" — and answering it shouldn't require manually adding up the value of every active opportunity. A pipeline that calculates the total value of open business (and, if you want, weighted by each stage's likelihood of closing) gives you that answer in real time, with no extra work.

## From won opportunity to invoice, without retyping anything

When a deal closes, generating the fee invoice shouldn't mean opening another program and retyping the client's name and the amount. If the pipeline and billing live in the same system, closing an opportunity can generate the corresponding document directly — one less step, one less chance of error.

## The essentials, in short

You don't need an expensive real estate CRM full of features you'll never use. What actually makes the difference is having contacts, opportunities, viewings, and billing connected to each other — so no opportunity gets lost simply because the information about it was scattered across three different places.
