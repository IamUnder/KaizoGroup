It's easy to assume a good invoicing program "already handles everything" — and that's a risky assumption. The reality is there's a clear line between what software can reliably automate (numbering, VAT calculation, categorizing income and expenses) and what requires up-to-date professional judgment (changing tax regulation, special regimes, obligations specific to your sector). Understanding that line avoids unpleasant surprises.

## What the software should handle

A properly generated invoice has correlative numbering with no gaps, the right VAT applied line by line, and is linked back to the quote or project it came from, where relevant. This isn't optional — it's the minimum baseline for any invoice to be valid and clear to whoever reviews it later, whether that's you, your client, or your accountant.

## An income-and-expense ledger that fills itself in

The part that saves the most time, day to day, is having every financial transaction in the business get categorized without anyone sorting it by hand: an invoice sent gets logged as a sale, a confirmed payroll run gets logged as a staff expense, a cash movement (rent, utilities) gets logged by type. At month end, instead of reviewing every line one by one, you have a ready-made summary of income, expenses, and results — ready to send to whoever handles your books.

## No double-entry bookkeeping, and that's fine

A small business doesn't need (and shouldn't try) to keep double-entry books itself — that's a professional's job. What it does need is a simplified version, like an income-and-expense ledger, that gives real visibility into the business without pretending to replace an accountant. The point is that the data comes out clean and traceable, not that the software "plays accountant."

## Credit notes: keep the thread, don't create loose documents

When an already-sent invoice needs to be cancelled or corrected, the credit note or correction should always be generated from the original document — that way it's clear what corrects what, without your accountant (or you, months later) having to reconstruct the relationship between two documents that otherwise look unrelated.

## Recurring billing: automate without losing control

If you invoice the same customers a monthly fee (maintenance, subscription, an ongoing service), generating that invoice by hand every month is repetitive work that adds nothing. The fix isn't fully automatic sending with no oversight — it's generating the draft automatically and letting you review it before it goes out, in case something needs adjusting that month.

## Where the software stops and your accountant starts

E-invoicing regulation and its technical requirements (Verifactu, SII, or your local equivalent) change, carry nuances depending on your tax regime and volume, and the consequences of getting it wrong are real. No management software should promise you it "already covers everything" without you confirming your specific situation with a tax advisor — the tool gives you clean, traceable data; specific regulatory compliance is, and should remain, a professional's job.
