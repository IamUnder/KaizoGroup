A small clinic — dental, physiotherapy, aesthetics, psychology — doesn't have the same problems as a hospital, but often ends up using (or trying to use) tools built for that scale, and pays for complexity it never touches. What a clinic actually needs day to day is much simpler: knowing who each patient is, what's been done for them before, when they're due back, and whether they owe anything.

## The patient record is the center of everything

When someone calls to book an appointment, whoever answers should be able to see, on the same screen: the name, phone number, past visits, and any relevant notes ("prefers afternoon slots", "allergic to X"). If that information lives on a paper card in a filing cabinet, every call starts with "one moment while I find your file" — and multiplied across every patient, every day, that's time you never get back.

## One calendar per practitioner, not a shared one you're guessing at

As soon as a clinic has more than one practitioner (two dentists, a physiotherapist and an osteopath, several beauticians), a single calendar that doesn't distinguish who's seeing whom is a recipe for chaos. The fix is a calendar of resources: each practitioner has their own configured time slots, and the system flags an appointment that falls outside their usual availability — without fully blocking it, because sometimes a real exception is needed (an emergency, a slot outside normal hours).

## Clear billing, without duplicating reception's work

When a consultation ends, generating the invoice or receipt shouldn't mean retyping the patient's name and the service from scratch — if the appointment and the patient are already in the system, the invoice just needs to inherit that data. And if the patient doesn't pay on the spot, the system should flag outstanding invoices without someone having to review a list by hand every week.

## "No-show" is useful data too

An appointment a patient doesn't show up for isn't just an entry that disappears from the calendar — it's a useful data point. If the system lets you mark it as "no-show" instead of just deleting it, over time you can see which patients miss more often, and decide whether it's worth reinforcing confirmation (a call, a reminder) with them.

## What belongs with your accountant, not your software

The specific tax-compliance side of e-invoicing (Verifactu, SII, or your country's equivalent) changes and has legal nuances that depend on your specific situation. Good management software gives you clean, organized data — correct invoice numbers, properly applied VAT, a clear income history — so you can hand it over without friction to whoever handles your tax advice. It isn't, and shouldn't try to be, a substitute for that advice.
